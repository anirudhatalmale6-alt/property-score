Break-even Headroom Modelled

A good arbitrage location is not the one with the highest nightly rate. It is the one where the gap between break-even and what you can actually fill is widest — cheap rent in a place people still want to stay. That gap is the whole business.

Year one is not steady state

A listing with no reviews does not fill like an established one. This model ramps the first year in quarters — 55%, 75%, 90%, then 100% of steady state — which averages to 80%. It is the line most spreadsheets quietly leave out, and it is usually the difference between a plan that works and one that runs out of cash in March.

        What this does not know

        It has no market data of its own. Every figure above is built from the comparables you typed in. That is deliberate for a demo, and it is also the honest position: the expensive part of a tool like AirDNA is not the maths, it is licensing or collecting millions of listings and their booking calendars. Plug a licensed market feed into the same engine and these inputs fill themselves — nothing else changes.

        Asking rates are not achieved rates. What a listing displays is not what it got. Without booking-calendar data, occupancy here comes from the market profile and your setup, not from watching those specific properties fill.

        The range is the answer, not the middle of it. Any tool that gives you one confident number from a handful of comps is overselling. The width above comes from how much your comps disagree and how many you gave.

        It is not tax, legal or lending advice. The flags are there to send you to the right question, not to answer it.

        Built as a working demonstration. The scoring engine is a single plain file with no dependencies, no API keys and no network calls — which is why the same maths can be run and tested outside the browser.
